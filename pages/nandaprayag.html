Panch Prayag • Uttarakhand, India

Nandaprayag

The confluence of the Alaknanda and Nandakini rivers

Nandaprayag is one of the five sacred river confluences known as the Panch Prayag of Uttarakhand. This page explains its rivers, source regions, geography, history and religious traditions.

1. Which Two Rivers Meet Here?

Alaknanda River + Nandakini River

2. Where Do the Rivers Originate?

The Alaknanda rises in the Satopanth and Bhagirath Kharak glacier region above Badrinath. The Nandakini drains high Himalayan snowfields and glacier-fed areas around the Nanda Ghunti–Trishul region.

3. What Is the River Called After the Confluence?

The Nandakini joins the Alaknanda, and the main river continues downstream as the Alaknanda.

4. Geographical Features

Nandaprayag is in Chamoli district in the Alaknanda valley. Steep mountain slopes and a settled valley corridor surround the confluence. Snowmelt, monsoon rainfall, landslides and sediment deposits influence the riverbanks and channel.

5. History and Religious Traditions

Local religious accounts associate the name with King Nanda. Nandaprayag is one of the five sacred confluences of Uttarakhand and is connected with pilgrimage, local temples and ritual bathing. Legends can vary among local and published traditions.

6. How Many Kilometres Does the Confluence Extend?

A standard official length or area for the sangam is not generally published. The visible meeting zone changes with river discharge, seasonal water levels, sediment bars and floods.

Measurement note: A confluence is a meeting point and a short river reach, not a feature with one universally fixed length. Its visible width and mixing zone can change with water level, monsoon flow, sediment and season. An exact kilometre figure should only be used when supported by a specific survey or authoritative source.
