Panch Kedar • Uttarakhand, India

Kalpeshwar Temple

Urgam Valley, Chamoli district, Uttarakhand

Kalpeshwar Temple is one of the five sacred Shiva shrines traditionally known as Panch Kedar. This page covers the deity, worship, beliefs, history and geographical setting.

1. Which Deity Is Worshipped Here?

Lord Shiva is worshipped at Kalpeshwar. In Panch Kedar tradition, Shiva's matted hair (jata) is believed to have appeared here.

2. Who Visits and What Do They Pray For?

Devotees worship Lord Shiva and pray for peace, protection, family welfare and spiritual blessings. Kalpeshwar is especially notable because it is traditionally accessible throughout the year, subject to local conditions.

3. Religious Beliefs, Vows and Legends

The Panch Kedar legend connects Kalpeshwar with Shiva's hair. Pilgrims may visit to fulfil personal vows or offer prayers, but the fulfilment of a vow is a matter of individual faith.

4. History and Sacred Traditions

Kalpeshwar is the fifth shrine in the Panch Kedar circuit in many traditional route descriptions. The shrine is associated with the Urgam Valley and is known for its cave-like setting. Local legends form part of its sacred history.

5. Temple Area and Dimensions

The shrine is a small cave-like temple. A reliable official measurement of the cave, temple footprint or total sacred precinct area in square metres is not consistently available, so no unsupported exact figure is given.

Measurement note: An exact area should only be stated when supported by an official temple record or architectural survey. No unsupported square-metre figure has been invented.

6. Geography and Access

Kalpeshwar lies in the Urgam Valley of Chamoli district. The route usually involves road travel toward Helang/Urgam followed by a short walk, but road access and walking conditions can change after rain, landslides or seasonal weather.

7. Worship Practices and Visitor Advice

Common observances include entering the shrine for darshan, prayers and offerings according to temple custom. Although traditionally open year-round, visitors should still confirm access and local conditions before travelling.
