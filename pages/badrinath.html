Panch Badri • Uttarakhand, India

Badrinath Temple

Badrinath, Chamoli district, Uttarakhand

Badrinath Temple is one of the sacred shrines traditionally included in the Panch Badri pilgrimage circuit. This guide covers the deity, worship practices, local beliefs, history and geographical setting.

1. Which Deity Is Worshipped Here?

Lord Badri Vishal, a form of Lord Vishnu, is the principal deity. The image is traditionally described as a black stone representation of Vishnu in a meditative posture.

2. Who Comes Here to Worship?

Devotees worship Lord Vishnu for spiritual protection, well-being, prosperity and liberation. The temple also honours associated figures and traditions, including Nara-Narayana and Lakshmi in the wider sacred setting.

3. Religious Beliefs and Vows

Pilgrims commonly pray for family well-being, peace, spiritual progress and blessings for ancestors. These are devotional practices and personal beliefs; specific results are matters of faith, not guarantees.

4. History and Sacred Traditions

Badrinath is one of the Char Dham sites of Uttarakhand and one of the important Vaishnava pilgrimage centres in India. Hindu tradition associates the site with Lord Vishnu's penance and with sages Nara and Narayana. The present temple has undergone repairs and rebuilding over time; legends and historical records should be distinguished.

5. Temple Area and Dimensions

There is no single agreed measurement describing the total area of the entire sacred complex, including temple buildings, courtyards and surrounding pilgrimage facilities. The temple structure and its precincts are much smaller than the wider Badrinath settlement and pilgrimage zone. Use an official survey or temple authority source if an exact area in square metres is required.

Measurement note: Exact temple area should only be stated when supported by a reliable temple authority, architectural survey or heritage record.

6. Geographical Setting

The temple stands in the Garhwal Himalaya beside the Alaknanda River, at roughly 3,100 metres above sea level. The high-altitude setting brings cold weather, snowfall and a short seasonal pilgrimage period.

7. Worship Practices and Visiting Advice

Common observances include darshan, puja, aarti, offerings of flowers and food, and ritual bathing at Tapt Kund where conditions and local rules permit. Opening and closing dates follow the temple's annual religious calendar.
