Karnaprayag

The confluence of the Alaknanda and Pindar rivers

Karnaprayag is one of the five sacred river confluences known as the Panch Prayag of Uttarakhand. This page explains its rivers, source regions, geography, history and religious traditions.

1. Which Two Rivers Meet Here?

Alaknanda River + Pindar River

2. Where Do the Rivers Originate?

The Alaknanda rises in the Satopanth and Bhagirath Kharak glacier region above Badrinath. The Pindar is fed by the Pindari Glacier region of the Kumaon Himalaya, near the Nanda Devi range.

3. What Is the River Called After the Confluence?

The Pindar joins the Alaknanda. Downstream from the confluence, the river continues to be called the Alaknanda.

4. Geographical Features

Karnaprayag lies in Chamoli district where water from the Pindar valley joins the Alaknanda system. Mountain slopes, river terraces, sediment deposits and strong seasonal changes in flow shape the landscape.

5. History and Religious Traditions

Tradition connects the name Karnaprayag with Karna from the Mahabharata, who is said to have performed penance here to please Surya. This is a religious legend, not a scientifically verified historical event.

6. How Many Kilometres Does the Confluence Extend?

No authoritative fixed kilometre length for the sangam itself is generally available. The visible confluence area can change as water levels, sediment bars and seasonal flow change.

Measurement note: A confluence is a meeting point and a short river reach, not a feature with one universally fixed length. Its visible width and mixing zone can change with water level, monsoon flow, sediment and season. An exact kilometre figure should only be used when supported by a specific survey or authoritative source.
