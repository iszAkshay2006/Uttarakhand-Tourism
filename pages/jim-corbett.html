Jim Corbett National Park

A practical guide to the scenery, viewpoints, local culture, best visiting season and useful travel information for Jim Corbett National Park, Uttarakhand.

Jim Corbett National Park offers a distinct experience of Uttarakhand's Himalayan landscapes and living heritage. Use this guide to plan what to see, when to go and how to visit respectfully.

Famous Viewpoints & Places

  • Dhikala landscape where access permits
  • Bijrani zone
  • Jhirna zone
  • Dhangarhi Museum and Ramganga views

Things to Do

Authorised jeep or canter safari, wildlife observation, birdwatching and interpretation.

Best Time to Visit

November–June is commonly used for many safari areas, but zone dates and access vary; monsoon closures may apply.

Weather, road access and opening dates can change. Check local advisories shortly before travelling.

Culture & Local Life

Local Kumaoni and Garhwali communities, conservation history and wildlife-protection culture.

History & Local Significance

Established in 1936 as Hailey National Park, it later became Jim Corbett National Park and played an important role in India's tiger-conservation history.

Population & Settlement

The protected park is not a town and has no single resident population figure; surrounding villages have their own census populations.

Population note: figures are not inserted where a reliable, comparable figure is unavailable. For official numbers, consult Census of India publications or the relevant municipal, village or forest authority, and state the year and administrative boundary.

Visitor Tips

Safari permits, zones and timings are regulated. Wildlife sightings are never guaranteed; follow guide instructions and never feed animals.

  • Carry reusable water bottles and take all litter back with you.
  • Respect local customs, worship areas, wildlife and private property.
  • Mountain weather can change quickly; keep flexible plans and suitable clothing.
  • Use authorised guides and operators for trekking, rafting, boating or safaris.

Responsible Travel

Choose locally run services where possible, ask permission before photographing people, and avoid disturbing wildlife or fragile natural areas. Do not treat local legends as verified historical facts unless a reliable historical source supports them.
