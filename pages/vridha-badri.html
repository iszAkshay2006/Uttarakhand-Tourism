Panch Badri • Uttarakhand, India

Vridha Badri Temple

Animath, near Joshimath, Chamoli district, Uttarakhand

Vridha Badri Temple is one of the sacred shrines traditionally included in the Panch Badri pilgrimage circuit. This guide covers the deity, worship practices, local beliefs, history and geographical setting.

1. Which Deity Is Worshipped Here?

The temple is dedicated to Lord Vishnu, worshipped here in the form known as Vridha Badri, traditionally understood as the aged or elder form of Vishnu.

2. Who Comes Here to Worship?

Devotees pray to Lord Vishnu for protection, peace, family well-being and spiritual blessings. The shrine forms part of the Panch Badri pilgrimage tradition.

3. Religious Beliefs and Vows

Local tradition says Vishnu appeared here in an aged form before the image was established. Devotees may visit to seek blessings and fulfil personal vows, but outcomes are matters of faith.

4. History and Sacred Traditions

Vridha Badri is traditionally included in the Panch Badri circuit. Religious accounts connect the site with the sage Narada and the appearance of Vishnu in an aged form. These are sacred traditions and should be distinguished from independently documented archaeological history.

5. Temple Area and Dimensions

The shrine is a small temple site. A verified, widely cited measurement of the temple building or total precinct area in square metres is not readily available; the page therefore avoids giving an unsupported number.

Measurement note: Exact temple area should only be stated when supported by a reliable temple authority, architectural survey or heritage record. No unsupported square-metre figure has been invented for this page.

6. Geographical Setting

The temple is located at Animath near Joshimath in the Garhwal Himalaya. Its lower elevation compared with Badrinath makes the approach different from the high-altitude main shrine, although local weather and road conditions still affect access.

7. Worship Practices and Visiting Advice

Typical worship includes darshan, prayers and offerings according to local temple practice. Ask local temple staff about current opening hours, festival days and any restrictions before travelling.
