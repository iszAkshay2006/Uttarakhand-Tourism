Rajaji National Park

Explore the natural beauty of Rajaji National Park, known for its elephants, diverse wildlife, birdlife and beautiful forest landscapes in Uttarakhand.

Rajaji National Park offers a distinct experience of Uttarakhand's Himalayan foothill landscapes and living heritage. Use this guide to plan what to see, when to visit and how to explore the park responsibly.

Best Time to Visit

The suitable visiting season and opening dates vary by park range. Check official forest department notices before planning a safari.

Weather, road access and safari availability can change. Check local advisories shortly before travelling.

Culture & Local Life

Communities around the park include Garhwali and other Himalayan foothill traditions. Conservation and human-wildlife coexistence are important local themes.

History & Local Significance

Rajaji National Park was formed in 1983 by combining protected forest areas and is named after C. Rajagopalachari. It protects important elephant corridors and diverse foothill habitats.

Wildlife & Biodiversity

Rajaji National Park is known for its rich biodiversity and forest ecosystems. The park provides habitat for Asian elephants, deer, leopards and many bird species. Its forests, grasslands and river ecosystems support a wide variety of wildlife.

Wildlife sightings are never guaranteed. Maintain a safe distance from animals and follow the instructions of authorised guides.

Population & Settlement

The park is a protected landscape rather than a single town. Nearby villages and settlements have separate populations and local administrative boundaries.

Safari & Park Experience

Visitors can explore permitted safari zones through authorised entry gates and approved operators. Safari availability, timings and routes depend on the forest department's current rules and seasonal conditions.

Always confirm safari availability, entry fees, booking requirements and permitted routes through official park authorities before visiting.

Visitor Tips

Use official entry gates and authorised guides. Do not leave designated routes, feed animals or enter closed forest areas.

  • Carry reusable water bottles and take all litter back with you.
  • Respect local customs, wildlife and private property.
  • Follow safari vehicle and guide instructions.
  • Avoid loud noises that may disturb wildlife.
  • Carry suitable clothing according to the season.
  • Use authorised guides and safari operators.

Responsible Travel

Choose locally run services where possible, ask permission before photographing people, and avoid disturbing wildlife or fragile natural areas. Do not feed animals, litter in the forest or attempt to approach wild animals for photographs.
