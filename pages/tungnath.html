Panch Kedar • Uttarakhand, India

Tungnath Temple

Near Chopta, Rudraprayag District, Uttarakhand

Home About History Geography Worship

Tungnath Temple is one of the five sacred Shiva shrines traditionally known as Panch Kedar. Located high in the Garhwal Himalayas, the temple is an important destination for pilgrims, nature lovers and mountain trekkers.

1. Which Deity Is Worshipped Here?

Lord Shiva is worshipped at Tungnath. According to the Panch Kedar tradition, the temple is associated with the arms of Shiva appearing at this sacred Himalayan location.

2. Who Visits and What Do They Pray For?

Pilgrims and trekkers visit to worship Lord Shiva and pray for peace, strength, protection and spiritual growth. The surrounding Himalayan landscape and smaller shrines are also important parts of the temple experience.

3. Religious Beliefs, Vows and Legends

According to the Panch Kedar legend, Lord Shiva appeared in different forms at five Himalayan locations after the Pandavas sought forgiveness following the Mahabharata war. This is a sacred religious tradition rather than independently verified historical evidence.

4. History and Sacred Traditions

Tungnath is traditionally considered one of the highest Shiva temples in the world and is an important Panch Kedar shrine. Its stone temple and surrounding smaller shrines reflect the religious heritage and architectural traditions of the Himalayas.

5. Temple Area and Dimensions

A single verified official figure for the total area of the temple building and its full precinct is not consistently available in public sources. The main temple is a compact stone shrine surrounded by a sacred complex.

Measurement note: An exact area should only be quoted when supported by an official record or architectural survey.

6. Geography and Access

Tungnath lies at approximately 3,680 metres above sea level. The usual walking route begins at Chopta and is roughly 3.5–4 km one way. On clear days, visitors can enjoy spectacular views of Himalayan peaks. Snow, rain and changing weather can make the route difficult or inaccessible.

7. Worship Practices and Visitor Advice

Typical observances include darshan, prayers, offerings and seasonal temple worship. Before visiting, check the temple's opening schedule and local trail conditions, especially during winter snowfall or heavy rain. Carry suitable walking shoes, warm clothing and water.
