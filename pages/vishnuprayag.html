Panch Prayag • Uttarakhand, India

Vishnuprayag

The confluence of the Alaknanda and Dhauliganga rivers

Vishnuprayag is one of the five sacred river confluences known as the Panch Prayag of Uttarakhand. This page explains its rivers, source regions, geography, history and religious traditions.

1. Which Two Rivers Meet Here?

Alaknanda River + Dhauliganga River

2. Where Do the Rivers Originate?

The Alaknanda rises in the Satopanth and Bhagirath Kharak glacier region above Badrinath. The Dhauliganga rises in the Niti Valley near the India–Tibet border.

3. What Is the River Called After the Confluence?

The Dhauliganga joins the Alaknanda. Downstream from the confluence, the main river continues to be called the Alaknanda.

4. Geographical Features

Vishnuprayag lies in a steep Himalayan valley in Chamoli district, near Joshimath. Rocky slopes, a narrow river channel, glacial meltwater and seasonal changes in sediment and flow shape the landscape.

5. History and Religious Traditions

The name is associated with Lord Vishnu. Local religious tradition links the site with sage Narada and worship of Vishnu. The Vishnu temple and Vishnu Kund are important parts of the sacred landscape; these accounts are religious traditions rather than geological evidence.

6. How Many Kilometres Does the Confluence Extend?

There is no widely accepted official kilometre measurement for the confluence itself. A sangam is a meeting point and short river reach, not a fixed-length feature. Its visible width and mixing zone vary with water level, season, sediment and monsoon flow.

Measurement note: A confluence is a meeting point and a short river reach, not a feature with one universally fixed length. Its visible width and mixing zone can change with water level, monsoon flow, sediment and season. An exact kilometre figure should only be used when supported by a specific survey or authoritative source.
