Vasudhara Falls

Discover the spectacular high-altitude waterfall near Mana and Badrinath, surrounded by the dramatic Himalayan scenery of Uttarakhand.

Vasudhara Falls offers a distinct experience of Uttarakhand's Himalayan landscapes and living heritage. Use this guide to plan what to see, when to go and how to visit respectfully.

  • Vasudhara Falls viewpoint

Things to Do

Trekking, waterfall views and visits to nearby pilgrimage and village sites.

Best Time to Visit

Usually late spring to early autumn, depending on snow, weather and local access.

Weather, road access and opening dates can change. Check local advisories shortly before travelling.

Culture & Local Life

Garhwali traditions and pilgrimage culture around Badrinath and Mana; local legends link the falls to the Mahabharata.

History & Local Significance

Vasudhara is associated with local religious legends, including Mahabharata-related traditions. These are beliefs and stories rather than established historical explanations.

Population & Settlement

The waterfall has no permanent population; Mana and nearby settlements are inhabited seasonally and year-round to varying degrees.

Population note: figures are not inserted where a reliable, comparable figure is unavailable. For official numbers, consult Census of India publications or the relevant village or forest authority, and state the year and administrative boundary.

Visitor Tips

The route is a hike at high altitude. Check local conditions, start early, carry water and avoid the trail in dangerous weather.

  • Carry reusable water bottles and take all litter back with you.
  • Respect local customs, worship areas, wildlife and private property.
  • Mountain weather can change quickly; keep flexible plans and suitable clothing.
  • Use authorised guides and operators for trekking and other activities.
  • Allow time to adjust to the high altitude and do not ignore symptoms of altitude sickness.

Responsible Travel

Choose locally run services where possible, ask permission before photographing people, and avoid disturbing wildlife or fragile natural areas. Do not treat local legends as verified historical facts unless a reliable historical source supports them.
