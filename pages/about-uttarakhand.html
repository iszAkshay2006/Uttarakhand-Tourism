Uttarakhand was formed on 9 November 2000 from the northern part of Uttar Pradesh. Its landscape ranges from Himalayan peaks and glacial valleys to forested foothills and plains. Garhwal and Kumaon are its two major cultural and administrative regions.

Land, Rivers & Climate

Geography

Uttarakhand lies in the central Himalaya. Its terrain includes high snow-covered mountains, glaciers, deep river valleys, mid-hill forests, the Shivalik foothills and southern plains. Elevation and weather can change sharply over relatively short distances, which shapes travel, farming, biodiversity and settlement patterns.

Location and boundaries

  • Located in northern India, with international borders adjoining China (Tibet Autonomous Region) and Nepal.
  • Shares domestic boundaries with Himachal Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh.
  • Much of the state is mountainous; the southern areas include plains and foothill landscapes.
  • The state is divided into Garhwal Division and Kumaon Division.

Rivers and ecosystems

  • The Bhagirathi and Alaknanda meet at Devprayag to form the Ganga.
  • The Yamuna rises in the Yamunotri region; many tributaries drain the Himalayan slopes.
  • Habitats range from subtropical forests to temperate woodland, alpine meadows and high-altitude environments.
  • Protected landscapes include Nanda Devi, Valley of Flowers, Gangotri, Govind and Jim Corbett areas, among others.

Weather and travel seasons

Conditions vary by altitude. Lower elevations can be hot in summer, while high mountain areas remain cold and may receive snow. Spring and autumn often offer comfortable conditions in many locations, but monsoon rain can trigger landslides, road closures and swollen rivers. Winter access to high-altitude destinations may be limited by snow and official closures.

People, Culture & Traditions

Heritage

Uttarakhand's heritage is shaped by mountain communities, pilgrimage traditions, local agriculture, seasonal festivals and connections to forests and rivers. Customs differ between districts, valleys and communities; visitors should avoid treating the state as culturally uniform.

Regions and languages

  • Garhwal: includes districts such as Dehradun, Haridwar, Uttarkashi, Tehri Garhwal, Pauri Garhwal, Rudraprayag and Chamoli.
  • Kumaon: includes Nainital, Almora, Pithoragarh, Bageshwar, Champawat and Udham Singh Nagar.
  • Hindi is widely used. Garhwali, Kumaoni, Jaunsari and other local languages and dialects are also part of the state's linguistic heritage.

Architecture, crafts and food

  • Traditional homes and temples reflect local materials, climate, craftsmanship and regional styles.
  • Local craft traditions include wool work, weaving, woodwork, metalwork and region-specific handcrafts.
  • Commonly associated foods include mandua (finger millet) roti, jhangora preparations, bhatt dishes, local dals and seasonal produce; recipes vary by household and region.
  • Folk music, dance, fairs and seasonal celebrations remain important expressions of community life.

Festivals and spiritual heritage

Harela, Phool Dei, Igas, Ghughutiya and Nanda-related traditions are among the region's cultural observances. Haridwar and Rishikesh are major spiritual destinations, while the Char Dham shrines—Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath and Badrinath—are important pilgrimage sites. Dates, access and rituals can vary, so check local guidance before attending.

Cultural courtesy: Ask before photographing people, ceremonies or private spaces. Follow temple rules, dress respectfully where requested and support local artisans and guides fairly.

Travel Thoughtfully

Responsible Travel

Mountain environments can be fragile and weather can change quickly. Responsible choices help protect local communities, wildlife, water sources and trails while making visits safer.

Safety and planning

  • Check weather forecasts, road status, permits, park rules and official advisories before departure.
  • During monsoon periods, be prepared for landslides, delays and sudden river-level changes; never cross flooded roads or streams.
  • For high-altitude walks, build in acclimatisation, carry suitable layers and use a registered guide where appropriate.
  • Tell someone your route and expected return time; keep offline maps and emergency contacts available.
  • Do not approach wildlife, feed animals or leave a vehicle in a protected area unless instructed.

Protect places and communities

  • Carry reusable bottles and take all rubbish back with you; do not leave plastic, food waste or cigarette ends.
  • Stay on marked paths and avoid picking plants, disturbing nests or removing natural and cultural objects.
  • Use water and electricity thoughtfully, especially in remote settlements.
  • Choose local guides, homestays and small businesses when possible, and agree on prices in advance.
  • Respect sacred sites, local customs, quiet zones and photography restrictions.

Important: This page is a general guide, not a live safety bulletin. Access rules, weather, permits, transport and opening dates change. Confirm current details with official departments, park authorities or local administration before travelling.

Quick Planning Checklist

  • Choose destinations suitable for your season, fitness and available travel time.
  • Check official route and access updates shortly before leaving.
  • Carry weather-appropriate clothing, water, basic first aid and essential medicines.
  • Keep cash for remote areas where digital payments or mobile networks may be unreliable.
  • Leave natural places clean and treat residents, wildlife and sacred spaces with respect.
