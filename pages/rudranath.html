Rudranath Temple

Near Gopeshwar, Chamoli District, Uttarakhand

Home About History Geography Worship

Rudranath Temple is one of the five sacred Shiva shrines traditionally known as Panch Kedar. Situated in the Garhwal Himalayas, the temple is known for its natural-rock shrine, alpine meadows and traditional pilgrimage routes.

1. Which Deity Is Worshipped Here?

Lord Shiva is worshipped at Rudranath as Neelkanth Mahadev. According to the Panch Kedar tradition, Shiva's face is believed to have appeared at this sacred location.

2. Who Visits and What Do They Pray For?

Pilgrims visit to worship Lord Shiva and pray for peace, protection, spiritual progress and blessings for their families. The temple is also visited as part of the Panch Kedar pilgrimage.

3. Religious Beliefs, Vows and Legends

The traditional Panch Kedar story associates Rudranath with Shiva's face. Devotees may undertake the demanding journey as an act of faith, penance or personal devotion. Spiritual outcomes are matters of religious belief.

4. History and Sacred Traditions

Rudranath is a natural-rock shrine within a high Himalayan landscape. Local sacred traditions, pilgrimage routes and seasonal worship are central to its religious heritage. Traditional accounts should be distinguished from independently documented historical evidence.

5. Temple Area and Dimensions

The shrine is a natural-rock temple rather than a large built complex. A reliable, standard official measurement of the temple footprint or entire sacred area in square metres is not readily available in the information used here.

6. Geography and Access

Rudranath lies in a Himalayan landscape of alpine meadows and forests. The pilgrimage involves a long mountain trek, with distances varying according to the starting point and route. Weather, altitude and trail conditions can make the journey challenging.

7. Worship Practices and Visitor Advice

Worship commonly includes darshan, prayer and offerings according to local temple customs. The temple is seasonal. Confirm opening dates, route conditions, guide availability and weather before departure. Carry appropriate trekking footwear, warm clothing, water and essential supplies.
