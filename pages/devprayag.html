Panch Prayag • Uttarakhand, India

Devprayag

The confluence where the Alaknanda and Bhagirathi form the Ganga.

Devprayag is one of the five sacred river confluences known as the Panch Prayag of Uttarakhand. This page explains its rivers, source regions, geography, history and religious traditions.

1. Which Two Rivers Meet Here?

Alaknanda River + Bhagirathi River = Ganga River

2. Where Do the Rivers Originate?

The Alaknanda rises in the Satopanth and Bhagirath Kharak glacier region above Badrinath. The Bhagirathi begins near Gaumukh at the Gangotri Glacier in Uttarkashi district.

3. What Is the River Called After the Confluence?

At Devprayag, the Alaknanda and Bhagirathi meet. Downstream from this confluence, the combined river is called the Ganga.

4. Geographical Features

Devprayag is in Tehri Garhwal district, where two major Himalayan headstreams meet in a deep valley. The rivers can appear different in colour because of variations in sediment and flow. The Ganga continues downstream toward Rishikesh and the plains.

5. History and Religious Traditions

Devprayag is an important pilgrimage town and is associated with the Raghunathji Temple. Local traditions connect the area with penance by Lord Rama and King Dasharatha. These are religious traditions, distinct from the geographical fact that the two rivers meet here.

6. How Many Kilometres Does the Confluence Extend?

The sangam itself has no universally accepted fixed length in kilometres. Its visible width and mixing zone vary with discharge, sediment and season. The key geographic fact is that the Alaknanda and Bhagirathi join here and the river is called the Ganga downstream.

Measurement note: A confluence is a meeting point and a short river reach, not a feature with one universally fixed length. Its visible width and mixing zone can change with water level, monsoon flow, sediment and season. An exact kilometre figure should only be used when supported by a specific survey or authoritative source.
