Panch Prayag • Uttarakhand, India

Rudraprayag

The confluence of the Alaknanda and Mandakini rivers

Rudraprayag is one of the five sacred river confluences known as the Panch Prayag of Uttarakhand. This page explains its rivers, source regions, geography, history and religious traditions.

1. Which Two Rivers Meet Here?

Alaknanda River + Mandakini River

2. Where Do the Rivers Originate?

The Alaknanda rises in the Satopanth and Bhagirath Kharak glacier region above Badrinath. The Mandakini rises from the Chorabari Glacier and surrounding snowfields above Kedarnath, with additional water from Himalayan tributaries.

3. What Is the River Called After the Confluence?

The Mandakini joins the Alaknanda. The river then continues downstream as the Alaknanda toward Devprayag.

4. Geographical Features

Rudraprayag is in the district of the same name, at the meeting of two Himalayan river valleys. The Mandakini drains the Kedarnath region. Steep terrain, heavy monsoon rain, landslides and flash-flood risk are important geographic factors.

5. History and Religious Traditions

The town's name is associated with Rudra, a form of Lord Shiva. It is one of the Panch Prayag and lies in a region connected with pilgrimage routes toward Kedarnath and Badrinath.

6. How Many Kilometres Does the Confluence Extend?

The confluence is a junction rather than a river reach with a fixed length. Its width and visible mixing area vary with season and river discharge; an official fixed kilometre figure is not generally specified.

Measurement note: A confluence is a meeting point and a short river reach, not a feature with one universally fixed length. Its visible width and mixing zone can change with water level, monsoon flow, sediment and season. An exact kilometre figure should only be used when supported by a specific survey or authoritative source.
